# Graf1 — Wildstyle Graffiti Style Guide (v2 — Gradient Aerosol)

> Rewritten from a closer re-read of reference piece `Graf_Style_01.jpg` on 2026-04-22.
> Key shift: real wildstyle lives in **aerosol gradient fades**, not flat fills. Background is now a **pure white studio isolation**, not a concrete wall.

## Style Definition

**Graf1 Wildstyle Burner v2** — a tightly-interlocking arrow-tipped wildstyle piece executed with **multi-stop aerosol gradient fills** (cadmium-yellow → burnt-orange bodies, cyan → deep-cobalt bodies), reinforced by a three-band outline stack (thin white inner highlight → thick cobalt primary outline → razor-thin navy-black keyline), lifted off pure white with a radial cyan overspray halo, weighted by a gradient-shaded 3D drop-block (cobalt → navy → near-black), and punctuated by white four-point star-burst highlights and a few tight drip accents. The entire piece floats on a clean pure-white background (#FFFFFF) — gallery / Samsung Frame isolation, NOT a street wall.

## Lineage DNA

- **New York Wildstyle Phase 2** (Dondi, Seen, Kase2, 1980s): interlocking arrow letterforms, crack highlights, multi-color fades
- **Writer Tradition** (Cope2, T-Kid, Ces, 1990s-2000s): bi-directional aerosol fades, three-band outline stacks, star-burst shines
- **European Wildstyle Evolution** (Does, Revok, Totem2, 2000s-2010s): candy-saturated gradient palettes, tightened letter construction, gradient 3D blocks
- **Studio-Isolated Product Shot** (Samsung Frame / gallery / Etsy listing aesthetic): piece photographed on clean white, floating composition, no environmental context

## The Core Shift — GRADIENTS, not flat fills

The biggest correction from v1: every major surface in a real burner is a **gradient**, executed by progressively releasing the cap while arcing across the letter body. A can held at one pressure for 5 seconds on the same area gives you a flat fill — that's the AMATEUR look. The pro move is a smooth fade from color A to color B (to sometimes C) across the letter body.

### Where gradients appear (EVERY one of these is a fade, not a flat fill)

1. **Letter body fills** — primary fade, 2-3 stops, one axis (usually top→bottom)
2. **3D drop-block** — secondary fade, cobalt → navy → near-black along the depth axis
3. **Cyan overspray halo** — radial fade, dense cyan at the letter silhouette, dissolving to pure white at the outer edge
4. **Drip accents** — each drip is gradient-matched to the letter it runs from (yellow→orange drip from a yellow letter)
5. **Optional inner glow** — a subtle inner highlight fade from the inner edge toward the letter center

### The gradient-stop lexicon

Use these exact stop descriptors when prompting — they map to spray-can colors that burner writers actually use:

| Palette | Stop 1 (top) | Stop 2 (mid) | Stop 3 (bottom) | Fade Direction |
|---------|--------------|--------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Solar Burner** (default) | Cadmium yellow #F8D12E | Burnt orange #EF7A1A | Crimson red #C8262E | Top → Bottom |
| **Ice Cobalt** | Pale cyan #7FDCF4 | Electric blue #1F5FD8 | Deep indigo #1A1F5C | Top → Bottom |
| **Emerald Wave** | Chartreuse #C2F042 | Emerald green #1FB26C | Deep teal #0C4F5C | Top → Bottom |
| **Magenta Heat** | Hot magenta #FF4DA8 | Violet #8A2DD0 | Deep purple #3A0F6E | Top → Bottom |
| **Flame Tongue** | White-hot #FFF8D9 | Cadmium yellow #F8D12E | Burnt orange #EF7A1A | Center → Edge (radial) |

Each fade uses 3 stops minimum. The transition zone between stops should occupy roughly the middle 30% of the letter body so the top and bottom are the "pure" stop colors.

## The Outline Stack — THREE bands, not one

Real wildstyle burners use a layered outline system. Gemini needs all three bands described explicitly or it will collapse them into a single fat outline.

| Layer | Color | Width (relative to letter height) | Position |
|-------|-------|-----------------------------------|----------|
| **Inner highlight** | Titanium white or pale cream #FEFDF2 | ~1-1.5% | Hugs the inside of the fill, just inside the primary outline |
| **Primary outline** | Saturated cobalt electric blue #1F5FD8 | ~3-4% | The main visible outline color, does all the compositional work |
| **Keyline shadow** | Deep navy-black #0A1530 | ~0.75-1% | Razor-thin shadow line just outside the primary outline |

### The rule

- Inner highlight is **always** present on the top and left edges of each letter (catching light) and fades out on the bottom-right. It is NOT a full loop.
- Primary outline is a **full loop** around every letter, even width all the way around.
- Keyline shadow is a **full loop** outside the primary outline, razor-thin — it's the hardest-hitting edge and what separates the piece from the background.

### Outline color swaps

When the fill palette changes, the outline stack can shift:

| Fill Palette | Inner Highlight | Primary Outline | Keyline |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| Solar Burner (yellow→red) | White | Cobalt blue | Navy black |
| Ice Cobalt (cyan→indigo) | White | Magenta hot-pink | Black |
| Emerald Wave (chartreuse→teal) | White | Hot orange | Black |
| Magenta Heat (pink→purple) | White | Lime green | Black |
| Flame Tongue (white→orange) | Pale yellow | Deep crimson | Black |

**Rule of thumb:** outline is the COMPLEMENT or high-contrast partner to the fill. Never the same family.

## The Background — PURE WHITE, full stop

### What changed from v1

v1 painted on "warm dark-grey concrete wall with grain, paint ghosts, dust and scuff, documentary street photography". **DELETE all of that.**

### The new rule (non-negotiable)

The piece floats on a **clean pure-white background (#FFFFFF)**. Studio-isolated product shot. Gallery / Samsung Frame TV / Etsy listing presentation.

**NO**:
- No wall texture (no concrete, no brick, no metal, no plywood)
- No environmental grit (no scuff, no dust, no paint ghosts)
- No floor, ground, horizon, or environmental shadow cast onto a surface
- No street context (no alley, no subway, no sidewalk)
- No lighting scene (no golden hour, no overcast daylight — the "light" is studio flat)

**YES**:
- Pure flat #FFFFFF white surrounding the letters on all sides
- Cyan overspray halo dissolves directly into the white
- Drips appear to float on the white surface (no ground plane)
- The composition is "isolated" the way a product photograph or vector sticker is isolated

### Prompt phrase to lock it in

> "Pure white background (#FFFFFF), studio isolation, no wall texture, no concrete, no grit, no environmental shadows, no street context, no floor, no horizon, the piece floats on clean flat white like a gallery print or Samsung Frame TV artwork or Etsy product listing."

## Letterform Philosophy (carried over from v1, still correct)

### Wildstyle Letter Construction

- **Interlocking, not side-by-side**: Letters weave into each other, arrows pierce adjacent counter-spaces, crossbars extend into neighbors' forms. One continuous ribbon of shapes, not a row of characters.
- **Arrow-tipped terminations**: Almost every letter extension ends in a triangular arrow. Arrows pointing up, down, diagonal — creating visual thrust.
- **Serif-extended flags**: Top and bottom of vertical letter strokes often extend into flag-shaped serifs or angular banners.
- **Shard counter-spaces**: The "holes" inside letters (counter-spaces) are carved into angular shards and slivers — never round bubbles, never soft curves.
- **Semi-illegibility is a FEATURE**: Wildstyle is designed to be hard to read for outsiders and read instantly by writers. Visual dialect, not a font.
- **No uniform baseline**: Letters tilt, rise, and compress at different heights along the piece. The baseline is a wave, not a line.

### Letterform vocabulary for prompts

- "wildstyle letterforms"
- "interlocking / interwoven / overlapping letters"
- "arrow-tipped terminations"
- "serif-extended flags"
- "shard-shaped counter-spaces"
- "angular geometric glyphs"
- "writer-tradition wildstyle"
- "semi-illegible abstract letterforms"
- "hand-painted aerosol lettering"

### Terms to AVOID in prompts

- "graffiti font" / "Urban font" / "street font"
- "spells X" / "reads X" / "the word X"
- "block letters" / "bubble letters"
- "neon sign" / "LED"
- "colorful graffiti" (too generic)
- "concrete wall" / "brick wall" / "street context" (background is WHITE now)

## Drip and Splatter Accents

The one organic-texture detail that survives the white-background shift. A few controlled drips add authenticity and prove this is aerosol work, not a vector render.

### Drip rules

- **Quantity**: 2-4 drips across the whole piece, NOT a curtain of mess
- **Origin**: Drips fall from an arrow tip, serif bottom, or letter base — never from the middle of a stroke
- **Length**: Short to medium — 5-12% of letter height
- **Color**: Each drip inherits the **bottom stop** of its parent letter's gradient (yellow letter → orange drip; cyan letter → indigo drip)
- **Drip head**: Small rounded bead at the tip, slightly wider than the drip shaft
- **Outline**: Drips have a thin primary-outline-color line around them, matching the main outline system (no inner highlight on drips, too small)

### Optional micro-splatter

A few (3-8) small paint-speck splatters can scatter around the letter edges where the can was at close range. Tiny dots, same colors as the fills, adding aerosol realism.

## Star-Burst and Crack Highlights

### Star bursts

- **Quantity**: 4-6 across the piece
- **Shape**: Classic four-point aerosol "shine" — two crossed lines with a small bright center, tips slightly tapered
- **Color**: Pure titanium white, occasionally a pale cream variant
- **Size variation**: At least 2 sizes — 2-3 large (3-4% of piece height) and 2-3 small (1.5-2%)
- **Placement**: ONLY at arrow tips, sharp serif corners, and 3D-block corners where light would hit
- **Never centered on flat areas** — they must sit on a geometric feature

### Crack highlights (new in v2)

- Small white hairline cracks or "lightning bolt" zigzags cutting across a fill, suggesting reflected highlight
- 2-3 per piece, each one tiny (5-10% of letter width)
- Placed on the top-facing part of the letter body
- White or pale-cream color, thin line

## The Cyan Halo (now radial-gradient, floating on white)

### What changed

In v1 the halo was a soft spray fading into dark concrete. In v2 the halo is a **radial gradient fading into white**. Same spray technique, different blend target.

### Halo rules

- **Shape**: Radial, extending 15-25% beyond the letter silhouette on all sides
- **Inner edge**: Dense saturated cyan (#56C8EA range), concentrated just outside the keyline shadow
- **Mid**: Soft cyan mist, desaturating
- **Outer edge**: Dissolves completely into pure white — NOT a grey edge, NOT a hard line
- **Irregularity**: The halo follows the letter silhouette loosely, slightly thicker in some spots, thinner in others — NOT a perfect circle

### Palette swaps for the halo

- Solar Burner → cyan halo (default)
- Ice Cobalt → magenta halo
- Emerald Wave → orange halo
- Magenta Heat → lime halo
- Flame Tongue → pale-yellow halo

---

## 3D Drop-Block (gradient-shaded in v2)

### What changed

v1 called for a "solid cobalt block, flat fill". v2 adds a **secondary gradient inside the 3D block itself**.

### v2 drop-block rules

- **Direction**: Consistent down-and-to-the-lower-left for the whole piece (never varying per letter)
- **Depth**: 5-10% of letter height, uniform across all letters
- **Gradient fill**: Cobalt #1F5FD8 at the top edge (where it meets the letter) → navy #0A1530 in the middle → near-black #050814 at the far edge
- **Outline**: The keyline shadow loops around the block edge too, same razor-thin navy-black
- **Small edge highlight**: A 1-pixel white hairline along the TOP edge of the block where it meets the letter — suggests a thin rim of reflected light

## Composition

### Format

**Horizontal banner** — wildstyle pieces fill horizontal space.

- **DEFAULT AND ONLY STANDARD**: 16:9 at 3840x2160px (Samsung Frame TV)
- 21:9 or 2:1 panoramic acceptable but non-default
- 1:1 / 9:16 are off-spec — wildstyle does NOT work vertical without major adjustment

### Layout Zones (on pure white)

```
+----------------------------------------------------+
|                                                    |
|     white space (breathing room)                   |
|                                                    |
|          CYAN HALO AURA (radial, → white)          |
|      +----------------------------------------+    |
|      |                                        |    |
|      |  INTERLOCKING WILDSTYLE LETTERS        |    |
|      |  (gradient fills, 3-band outlines,     |    |
|      |   gradient 3D blocks, star bursts)     |    |
|      |                                        |    |
|      +----------------------------------------+    |
|       /drip  /drip              /drip              |
|                                                    |
|     white space                                    |
+----------------------------------------------------+
             pure white background (#FFFFFF)
```

### Placement Rules

- **Letters fill 60-75% of the width** — leaves margin for halo + breathing white
- **Letters occupy the middle 45-65% of the height** — halo and white space live above/below
- **Slight asymmetry** — minor rightward or leftward lean feels more authentic than centered
- **No handstyle footer tags in v2** (they reintroduced a "street" feel — v2 is gallery-isolated)
- **3D drop-block extends down-left** beyond the main letter bounds, becoming part of the compositional weight
- **Drips hang down** from arrow tips, floating on the white — no ground plane

### Eye Flow

1. Enter through the cyan halo periphery (soft visual anchor)
2. Lock onto the brightest fill color (usually the top of a Solar Burner yellow)
3. Ride the interlocking ribbon across the piece
4. Follow the 3D drop-block down and to the left
5. Return to admire star-burst sparkles and drip details

## Mood

**Candy-saturated, technically flawless, confident — a gallery-grade burner with product-photograph polish.**

- **Saturated** — the gradients are POPPY, not muted. Full-chroma cadmium, electric cobalt, hot magenta.
- **Technical** — clean edges, consistent 3-band outlines, deliberate gradient stops, intentional star bursts.
- **Confident** — seasoned-writer line quality, no wobble, no hesitation.
- **Gallery** — isolated on white, presentation-ready, not a street document.
- **Modern** — this is 2020s digital-native wildstyle, evolved from tradition but styled for screens and walls in clean interiors.
