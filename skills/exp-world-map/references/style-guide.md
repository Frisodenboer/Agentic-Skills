# Abstract-Expressionist — Style Guide

> Extracted from reference painting analysis on 2026-04-11

## Style Definition

**Abstract Expressionist Color-Field Composition** — non-representational paintings built from interlocking organic and geometric color shapes in saturated triadic harmonies, with visible gestural impasto brushwork, Hofmann-style push-pull color dynamics creating spatial depth without perspective, and an energetic all-over composition that distributes visual interest across the entire canvas.

## Art Movement DNA

- **Abstract Expressionism** (de Kooning, Kline, 1940s–1960s): bold gestural brushwork, large-scale energy, interlocking forms oscillating between structure and chaos, visible hand of the painter celebrated
- **Hans Hofmann's Push-Pull Theory** (1950s–1960s): spatial depth created through color temperature alone — warm colors advance, cool colors recede — no linear perspective needed
- **Color-Field Painting** (Rothko, Frankenthaler, 1950s–1960s): large saturated color areas as emotional vehicles, staining and pouring techniques, luminous color interactions
- **Lyrical Abstraction** (Joan Mitchell, 1950s–1970s): gestural energy combined with poetic color harmonies, landscape-derived palettes in non-representational form, visible joy in paint handling
- **Fauvism** (Matisse, Derain, 1905–1908): unmixed high-chroma color placed directly on canvas, color as emotional expression rather than description, bold non-naturalistic choices

### Color Strategy
- **Harmony**: Primary triadic (red/yellow/blue) as the foundation, with complementary tension at red-green and blue-orange axes creating optical vibration at boundaries
- **Temperature**: Warm dominant (~60/40 warm-to-cool ratio). Warm colors carry the energy; cool colors provide counterpoint and spatial recession
- **Value range**: Full range from deep raw umber (Value 3) through saturated mid-tones (Value 5-7) to titanium white (Value 10). Darks used sparingly as anchors, lights used strategically as energy sources
- **Saturation hierarchy**: Highest chroma colors (Cadmium Red, Lime Green, Cerulean Blue) are the visual engines. Medium chroma (Burnt Sienna, Viridian) provides structural support. Low chroma (Raw Umber, Slate Gray) provides visual rest
- **Push-pull rule**: Every saturated warm passage should have a cool counterpoint nearby. This creates the Hofmann spatial vibration — the illusion that some shapes float forward while others recede
- **Neutral breathing room**: Saturated zones must be separated by neutral passages (earth tones, grays, off-whites). Without these "rest areas," the composition becomes visually exhausting

## Brushwork & Technique

| Zone | Stroke Style | Paint Application | Texture |
|------|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| Light/White Passages | Bold confident sweeps, visible bristle tracks, flick endings | Thick opaque impasto, heavy body, loaded brush | Highest — raised ridges, physical relief, most tactile |
| Saturated Color Shapes | Broad directional strokes following form contours, varied angles | Medium-thick impasto, palette knife on select shapes | High — each shape has its own stroke direction and surface quality |
| Dark Passages | Shorter, broken marks, thin fluid application | Thinned paint, semi-transparent, fluid washes | Low — smoother, flatter, physically receding |
| Color Boundaries | Palette knife scraping, clean edges alongside soft blending | Mixed — some sharp knife cuts, some wet-on-wet merges | Varied — deliberate contrast between sharp and soft edges |
| Transitional Zones | Scumbled dry-brush drag, stippled dabs, partial blending | Dry brush over underlying layers, broken opaque marks | Moderate — textural interest without heavy buildup |

### Edge Strategy
- **Sharp defined edges**: Where palette knife was used, where thick paint laid over dried layer — angular geometric shapes, deliberate form boundaries
- **Soft blurred edges**: Where colors blended wet-on-wet — warm passages (oranges into reds), lyrical flowing transitions
- **Lost-and-found edges**: Edges that appear and disappear along a form's contour — white shapes dissolving into adjacent light tones, creating ambiguity
- **Scumbled edges**: Dry-brush dragged across color boundaries creating transitional shimmer without full blending
- **The edge variation IS the composition**: Constant alternation between sharp and soft prevents visual monotony and guides the eye through rhythmic tension and release

### Signature Techniques
1. **Push-pull color dynamics** — warm advancing colors placed beside cool receding colors, creating spatial depth through temperature contrast alone
2. **Thick impasto lights / thin fluid darks** — lighter passages physically project from the canvas surface while darker passages lie flat, creating actual 3D spatial variation
3. **Interlocking organic-geometric forms** — sweeping curves intersected by sharp angular planes, lyrical flow disrupted by geometric tension
4. **All-over composition** — visual energy distributed across every square inch, no "background" zones, every region contains active color relationships
5. **Complementary vibration** — red/green and blue/orange boundaries create optical buzz, the visual equivalent of a musical dissonance that resolves
6. **Scumbled dry-brush transitions** — broken opaque marks dragged over underlying layers create optical depth without physical buildup

## Composition — Creating Dynamic Abstract Energy

### Format
**Non-representational all-over composition** — works well in landscape (4:3, 16:9) and square (1:1) formats. Landscape orientation creates panoramic energy fields; square creates intimate, concentrated compositions.

### The 9 Composition Rules

These rules transform flat color shapes into compositions with genuine visual power. Include the relevant rules explicitly in generation prompts.

#### 1. Push-Pull Dynamics — Hofmann's Spatial Engine
Create depth through color temperature: warm saturated colors advance, cool colors recede. This creates spatial vibration on a flat surface — the foundational technique of this style. Without it, the composition collapses into a flat decorative pattern.
- **Advancing**: Cadmium Red, Cadmium Yellow, Tangerine Orange, Peach
- **Receding**: Cerulean Blue, Ultramarine, Viridian, Slate Gray
- **Bridge colors**: Lime Green (can push or pull depending on context), Magenta (transitional)

#### 2. All-Over Composition — No Dead Zones
Energy distributed across the entire canvas. No single focal point, no inert background zones. Every region contains active color relationships, gestural marks, and visual interest. The eye circulates rather than settling.

#### 3. Form Tension — Curves Against Angles
Interlocking curvilinear organic forms with sharp angular geometric planes. Curves provide lyrical flow and movement; angles provide disruptive energy and visual punctuation. Their collision creates the tension that keeps the eye engaged.

#### 4. Value Anchoring — Darks Ground the Composition
Strategic deep dark passages (raw umber, dark ultramarine, deep maroon) anchor the composition and prevent it from floating. Without darks, even vibrant colors feel unmoored and the painting lacks gravity. Darks should occupy 10-20% of the total area.

#### 5. Luminous Activation — Whites as Energy Sources
Bright white and pale cream shapes act as "energy sources" — areas where the eye rests and resets. They create the highest contrast against saturated masses and activate negative space. These are deliberate luminous events, not empty zones. Should occupy 10-15% of total area.

#### 6. Complementary Vibration — Color Buzz
Complementary colors (red/green, blue/orange) placed in direct adjacency create optical vibration at their boundary — a visual "buzz" that generates energy without physical movement. This is the engine of visual excitement in abstraction.

#### 7. Varied Surface — Texture as Composition
Thick impasto ridges on light passages vs thin fluid washes on darks. The physical texture variation becomes compositional information: thick = advancing, thin = receding. This reinforces the push-pull dynamics with actual surface relief.

#### 8. Rhythmic Color Echo — Visual Rhymes
Specific hues repeated at multiple locations in varying scales and intensities. A vivid green upper-right, a muted version lower-left. These color echoes create visual rhymes that unify the composition across the full canvas.

#### 9. Density Gradient — Gravitational Pull
Subtle variation in visual density and paint weight across the canvas. Lighter/airier passages above, heavier/denser paint below — creating natural gravitational pull that mirrors physical experience and gives the composition a sense of physical presence.

### Structure
- **Interlocking mosaic** — shapes overlap and interweave like a jigsaw puzzle of color, no single dominant form
- **Push-pull layering** — warm shapes advance forward, cool shapes recede back, creating illusion of depth without perspective
- **Rhythmic alternation** — warm/cool/warm/cool across the surface creates visual rhythm, like a color melody
- **Structural punctuation** — dark anchoring passages and white energy sources placed asymmetrically to prevent uniformity

### Focal Strategy
Unlike landscape painting with a single focal point, this style uses **distributed attention**. The eye is pulled in multiple directions simultaneously by the push-pull dynamics, then allowed to rest briefly at luminous white/cream zones before being drawn back into the saturated field.

## Lighting

### Type
**Diffused, omnidirectional studio light** — no directional light source, no cast shadows, no golden hour. The illumination is even and ambient, like a well-lit gallery.

### Critical Insight
**Drama comes from color contrast, not chiaroscuro.** This is fundamental to the style. Do not include lighting instructions (backlighting, spotlight, dramatic shadows) in prompts — they work against the abstract expressionist aesthetic. The painting's energy is entirely chromatic.

### Value Strategy
- **Saturated mid-tones** (Value 5-7): Do the heavy lifting — Cadmium Red, Lime Green, Cerulean Blue carry the composition
- **Dark passages** (Value 3-4): Raw Umber, Dark Ultramarine — used sparingly as anchors (10-20% of area)
- **Light passages** (Value 8-10): Titanium White, Warm Ivory — used strategically as energy sources (10-15% of area)
- **The majority of the canvas** lives in saturated mid-tones, bookended by darks below and lights above

## Mood

**Vital, energetic, exuberant — structured chaos with deliberate control beneath apparent spontaneity.**

- **Vital** — the high-chroma colors and visible gestural brushwork convey creative force and life energy
- **Energetic** — the push-pull dynamics and complementary vibrations create constant visual movement
- **Exuberant** — the saturated, unapologetic color choices suggest confidence and celebration
- **Structured** — beneath the apparent spontaneity lies deliberate compositional control — every color placement, every edge decision serves the whole
- **Optically stimulating** — the painting rewards sustained looking, with new relationships and rhythms revealing themselves over time

### Customization Variables

When generating new images in this style, these elements can be varied while maintaining the style:

- **Palette family**: Primary triadic (red/yellow/blue), Warm dominant (reds/oranges/golds with blue-green accents), Cool dominant (blues/greens/violets with warm accents), Earth tones (siennas/ochres/umbers with selective chroma), Jewel tones (deep ruby/sapphire/emerald with gold accents), Muted palette (desaturated with selective vivid accents)
- **Energy level**: Calm/meditative (larger shapes, softer edges, closer values), Moderate (balanced variety of forms and edges), Explosive/chaotic (fragmented shapes, sharp edges, extreme value contrasts)
- **Form emphasis**: Organic curvilinear (flowing, lyrical, Mitchell-inspired), Angular geometric (structured, architectural, more rigid), Mixed interlocking (curves colliding with angles, de Kooning-inspired)
- **Surface quality**: Heavy impasto (maximum physical texture, almost sculptural), Moderate texture (visible brushwork but controlled), Smooth glazes (thinner paint, more luminous, Rothko-adjacent)
- **Color temperature**: Warm dominant (60/40 warm-cool), Cool dominant (40/60 warm-cool), Balanced (50/50)
- **Accent elements**: Metallic gold/copper accents, Neon-bright accents against muted grounds, Muted earth passages between saturated zones, Black structural accents instead of dark brown
- **Scale**: Intimate (smaller shapes, more detail), Monumental (fewer larger shapes, more impact), Medium (balanced variety)
