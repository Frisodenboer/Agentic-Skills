# Adobe Stock — Generative AI Content Submission Standards (source policy)

> Verbatim reference copy of Adobe's public contributor guidance on submitting generative-AI-made content to Adobe Stock. Kept here for deep lookups; `SKILL.md` has the condensed, actionable checklist derived from this document.

Adobe Stock accepts content made using generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools as long as it meets submission standards.

## 1. Ensure you have the appropriate rights to submit

You must have all necessary rights to submit generative AI images, vectors, or videos to Adobe Stock for licensing as described in Adobe's Contributor Terms (e.g., broad commercial use). Review the terms of any generative AI tools used to confirm the right to submit content created with them to Adobe Stock for licensing.

Generative AI content prompts, titles, or keywords:

- May **not** contain names of artists, real known people, fictional characters, or references to creative works still in copyright.
- May **not** contain names of people.
- May **not** imply the content is a depiction of an actual newsworthy event.
- May **not** contain names of government agencies.
- May **not** contain references to third-party intellectual property.

Violating this policy may result in content removal or account termination.

## 2. Label content as Generative AI prior to submission

- Check the **"Created using generative AI tools"** checkbox in the Adobe Stock contributor portal before submitting any content created with generative AI software.
- Check the **"People and Property are fictional"** box if the image or video features a fake person or fake property.

### Does Generative Fill / Generative Expand / Generative Recolor require the GenAI label?

Label as generative AI when the use of these tools **changed, augmented, or added a new primary subject** of an image:

- Generating new additions (a new person, animal, or object)
- Adding new subjects that might compromise the accuracy of an image
- Making significant adjustments/changes to a human subject
- Recoloring the primary subject

**Not** required to label when:

- Extending background for any reason
- Removing IP or other retouching
- Removing distracting objects or people
- Recoloring the background

## 3. Select the accurate asset type, and add title and keywords

- **Photos** — if it looks camera-captured and features photo-realistic subjects that exist in real life, respecting human/animal anatomy.
- **Illustrations** — if it features artistic illustration or fantasy concepts (imaginative people, food, animals, environments not necessarily seen in real life).
- Generative AI **video** can only be submitted as asset type **Videos**.
- Generative AI **vectors** can only be submitted as asset type **Vector**.

**Do:**
- Include the main subject of the prompt in the title.
- Include the individual words/concepts from the title in the top 10 keywords for a search-relevance bump.

**Don't:**
- Add "generative AI" to titles or keywords — the "Created by using generative AI tools" checkbox already classifies the asset.
- Keep repetitive technical parameters (platform-specific features, weights, settings) from the prompt in the asset title.

Same rights restrictions as Section 1 apply to titles/keywords: no artist names (work still in copyright), no people's names, no implied newsworthy events, no government agency names, no third-party IP references.

## 4. Model and property release requirements for generative AI content depicting people

- Any GenAI content that depicts, is based on, or is intended to portray an **identifiable real person** requires a model release (e.g., a real person's photo was used as a prompt input, or a specific individual is named in the prompt).
- If the content was **not** based on a real person but visually resembles one, check the **"People and Property are fictional"** box to certify this — no release needed in that case.
- If there are no recognizable people/property at all, no release is required and the fictional box may be left unchecked.
- **Always** check "Created using generative AI tools" regardless of release status, for all GenAI content.

**Do:** upload model releases for GenAI assets depicting real people; upload property releases for GenAI assets depicting real property.

**Don't:** submit work created with prompts referring to real people, places, or property without the legal right to do so (e.g., other artists' names, notable people, famous characters/brands).

Generative AI content **cannot** be submitted to the Illustrative Editorial Collection (IEC) — IEC is for accurate, unmodified, editorial-use portrayals of brands only.

## 5. Prioritize quality

**Do:**
- Check submissions carefully so anatomy is intended and relevant (GenAI commonly flubs hands, faces, limb counts).
- Use GenAI tools to fill genuine content needs within the collection.
- Select only images/vectors/videos that provide unique value to the collection.

**Don't:**
- Use an image/vector/video you don't have the rights to as a generation parameter/reference.
- Describe or depict subjects/locations in ways that may mislead buyers.
- Submit multiple versions from the same prompt or near-identical iterations (spam policy).

## 6. Curate intentionally (GenAI-specific)

AI-generated content opens up creative opportunities, but it demands careful prompting and curation. Submitting dozens of variations from a single prompt with only minimal changes between them can lead to content being refused for being too similar.

**Tips for distinct AI-generated submissions:**

- Focus on **concept diversification**, not just visual changes — a genuinely different idea, not the same idea re-rendered.
- Don't mass-upload all of a prompt's outputs. Review the batch and select only the strongest piece(s).
- Ensure content is commercially viable and free of artifacts or inaccurate information.
- Use metadata and titles that clearly reflect what's actually unique about each submitted piece.

**Variations to avoid** (these read as spam even when each individual file looks fine on its own):

- Flipped or rotated versions of the same image
- Different image filters applied to the same underlying image
- Different background colors or minor color variations
- Similar composition or subject without substantive conceptual change
- Different cropping or aspect ratio of the same image

---

Non-compliant content may be removed, or the contributor account deactivated.

## Illustrative Editorial Collection (IEC) — separate content track

> Source: Adobe's public contributor guidance on illustrative editorial submissions. This is a **distinct track from generative AI submissions** — Section 4 above already states GenAI content cannot be submitted to IEC at all; this section covers IEC's own rules for the non-AI content it does accept.

### What is illustrative editorial content?

Conceptual imagery designed to illustrate articles on current events and newsworthy topics. It often features images of real brands and products — signs on buildings, soda cans, computers, cars — to convey a story. Made available to Adobe Stock customers for **editorial use only**.

Illustrative editorial is **not** the same as traditional editorial content (which documents events/incidents currently occurring, developing, or already occurred) — Adobe does not accept traditional editorial content at all.

**IEC does not accept:**

- Images that feature recognizable people
- Images of restricted events such as conventions and sports games
- Images that feature tight crops of copyrighted or trademarked material (stamps, fine art, or other content that may violate privacy rights)
- Digitally created or manipulated versions of trademarked logos or other brand content — other than social media icons
- Collage

### Who can submit illustrative editorial?

Only contributors with **100 or more downloads** in their account history. Eligible contributors see a **"This is Illustrative Editorial content"** checkbox in the Uploaded Files tab — its presence is itself the indicator of eligibility.

### Tech specs and best practices

- Accepted formats: photos, illustrations, vector images. **No video** for illustrative editorial.
- Standard photo/illustration and vector file-format and min/max size requirements apply.
- Content must be properly exposed and of highest technical quality.
- Only post-process or crop if it does **not** change the context or meaning of the content.

### Submission steps

1. Upload files in the Adobe Stock Contributor portal.
2. In the Uploaded Files tab, check **"This is Illustrative Editorial content"** — required every time, for every submission.
3. Write a title that accurately describes the content **including date and location**.
   - Example: "General view of the Funko headquarters sign under dark clouds on February 2, 2019 in Everett, Washington."
4. Add **5-50 keywords** that accurately describe what's in the image.
   - The first 10 keywords carry more search weight — list the most important ones first, starting with any included brand names.
   - Include the capture location if relevant.
   - Example: Shell, gas, station, price, sign, fuel, business, service, gasoline, business, car, travel, economy, oil, auto, automobile, advertisement, company, Everett, Washington.
5. Submit for review.

Contributors with an existing illustrative-editorial portfolio hosted elsewhere can request consideration via the "Contact Us" link at the bottom of the Adobe Stock Contributor portal.
