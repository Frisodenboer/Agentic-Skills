# Pastoral-Frame — Style Guide

> Extracted from reference painting on 2026-04-05

## Style Definition

**Post-Impressionist Patchwork Pastoral Landscape** — rural agricultural landscapes at dusk rendered as vibrant mosaics of color-field patches stretching toward distant mountains, with expressive non-naturalistic color, visible impasto brushwork, and a dreamlike quality of serene nostalgia.

## Art Movement DNA

- **Post-Impressionism** (Cezanne, Van Gogh, 1880s–1900s): structured directional brushwork, expressive color liberated from observation, geometric simplification of natural forms into planes and facets
- **Fauvism** (Matisse, Derain, 1905–1908): bold non-naturalistic color choices driven by emotion rather than fidelity, flat unmodulated color areas, decorative approach to landscape
- **Pointillism / Divisionism** (Seurat, Signac, 1880s–1890s): broken color laid in discrete patches, optical mixing in the viewer's eye, systematic approach to color application
- **Japanese Woodblock Prints** (Hiroshige, Hokusai): flat color zones with minimal shading, strong horizon-dominated compositions, atmospheric depth through layered planes rather than perspective
- **Contemporary Pastoral Color-Field Landscape** (Wolf Kahn, April Gornik): luminous pastoral scenes built from overlapping color fields, pastel-and-saturated juxtapositions, landscape as emotional vehicle

### Color Strategy
- **Harmony**: Complementary juxtaposition — warm orange/terracotta fields paired against cool sage/lime greens, pink pastures against blue-violet mountains. The patchwork itself IS the color harmony system.
- **Temperature**: Warm dominant in foreground fields, gradually cooling through midground (sage greens, blue-grays) to cool mountains and sky. Temperature gradient reinforces depth.
- **Value range**: Full range from dark charcoal-green structural accents through luminous mid-tone patches to near-white sky highlights. Greater value contrast in foreground, compressed values in background.
- **Patchwork rule**: Each field patch should hold a distinct hue identity. Neighboring patches should contrast — warm beside cool, saturated beside muted — creating the mosaic effect. Avoid smooth blending between patches; instead use visible, gestural borders.
- **Optical mixing**: Individual patches read as discrete colors at close range but merge into a unified landscape at viewing distance. The eye completes the scene.

## Brushwork & Technique

| Zone | Stroke Style | Paint Application | Texture |
|------|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| Sky | Broad horizontal sweeps with soft wrist-flicks at edges | Thin translucent glazes, wet-into-wet blending at zone boundaries | Smooth, atmospheric, near-enamel finish |
| Field Patches | Short directional strokes following patch contours, varied angles per patch | Thick impasto, palette knife scraping on some patches, loaded brush on others | High — each patch has its own tactile surface quality |
| Trees/Structures | Compact vertical strokes, small dab-and-lift marks | Dense impasto, dark pigment applied thickly for maximum contrast | Rugged, raised from surface, highest relief |
| Mountains | Horizontal banding strokes, slightly broken | Medium-body paint, semi-opaque layering | Moderate — visible stroke directionality, softer than fields |
| Field Borders/Edges | Crisp gestural strokes defining patch boundaries | Medium impasto, deliberate mark-making | Defined ridges where patches meet |

### Edge Strategy
- **Hard edges**: Tree silhouettes against sky or fields — crisp dark shapes for maximum contrast
- **Soft edges**: Sky gradients — fully feathered transitions from horizon warmth to upper cool
- **Lost-and-found**: Mountain ridges dissolving into atmospheric haze at the far distance
- **Interlocking**: Field patch boundaries — deliberately irregular, not ruler-straight, suggesting hand-plowed irregularity
- **Diffused**: Distant structures and trees, softened by atmospheric perspective

### Signature Techniques
1. **Impasto patchwork mosaic** — thick, distinct color patches laid side by side, each with visible brush direction, creating a tactile quilt-like surface
2. **Color temperature sequencing** — warm-to-cool gradient from foreground to background, each "row" of patches slightly cooler than the last
3. **Structural dark accents** — strategically placed dark trees and structures anchor the composition and provide value contrast against the vivid patchwork
4. **Scumbled field borders** — dry-brush dragging at patch boundaries creates a transitional shimmer without fully blending
5. **Glazed sky luminosity** — thin transparent layers build up sky glow, contrasting with the opaque thick field patches below
6. **Palette knife highlights** — select field patches receive a scrape of pure color across the top, catching light physically on the canvas surface

## Composition — Making Artwork Addicting to Look At

### Format
**Single-field panoramic landscape** — wide aspect ratio emphasizing the horizontal sweep of patchwork fields. The panoramic breadth allows the mosaic pattern to fully develop across the canvas. Encourage 16:9 or 21:9 aspect ratios.

### The 9 Composition Rules

These rules transform a pleasant landscape into a painting the eye can't leave. Include the relevant rules explicitly in generation prompts — Gemini responds to named compositional techniques.

#### 1. Rule of Thirds — Power Points, Not Center
Place the primary focal point (mountain peak, village, tallest tree) at an **upper-left or upper-right intersection** of the thirds grid. Never dead center. This creates dynamic visual tension and gives the foreground its own weight.
- Vertical zones: **Top third** = sky with dusk gradient, **Middle third** = distant mountains, scattered tree lines, far-field patches, **Bottom third** = foreground patchwork fields in richest, warmest, most saturated colors

#### 2. Leading Lines — Pull the Eye Inward
Use valley floors, field borders, ridgelines, fence lines, tree rows, and waterways to create **converging diagonal lines** toward the focal point. Diagonal lines are more dynamic than horizontal ones. The patchwork field boundaries themselves become natural leading lines.

#### 3. Foreground Framing — Layered Depth
Build the scene as **3-4 distinct depth layers**: immediate foreground (richest colors, largest strokes), midground (trees, structures, cooler tones), far distance (mountains, mist), and sky. The foreground wraps around the viewer like a frame, creating irresistible depth. Dark trees on either side create a natural window effect.

#### 4. Atmospheric Perspective — Warm to Cool Gradient
Progressive color shift from **warm saturated foreground** to **cooler, mistier, less saturated distance**. Each layer is slightly cooler, lighter, and softer than the one before it. This is the #1 technique for convincing depth:
1. **Color temperature recession** — warm colors advance, cool colors recede
2. **Value compression** — higher contrast in foreground, compressed tonal range in background
3. **Size diminution** — trees and structures shrink with distance
4. **Atmospheric softening** — distant mountains are lighter, cooler, less saturated, edges softened
5. **Brushwork scaling** — larger, more textured strokes in foreground; smaller, smoother strokes in background
6. **Overlap** — foreground patches overlap midground elements, which overlap mountains, which overlap sky

#### 5. Color Harmony with Tension — Warm vs Cool
Create a dominant warm-cool contrast (golden fields against blue-mauve mountains). Then add a **small accent of complementary color** (vivid orange or magenta in the sky, a single bright blue patch in warm fields). Pure harmony is boring — tension keeps the eye engaged.

#### 6. Varied Rhythm — Break the Pattern
Alternate large and small patches, thick and thin strokes, bright and muted colors. The eye should never settle into a monotone rhythm. Create visual "surprises" — a bright terracotta patch here, a dark tree cluster there, a sudden lime accent. Think jazz, not metronome.

#### 7. Tonal Contrast — Dark Against Light
Strong dark-against-light contrasts at key focal areas: mountain silhouette against luminous sky, dark tree silhouettes against golden fields, deep shadow beside bright highlight. Without strong value contrast, a painting feels flat regardless of color.

#### 8. Edge Control — Soft Distance, Sharp Foreground
- **Hard edges**: Tree silhouettes against sky or fields — crisp dark shapes for maximum contrast (foreground)
- **Soft edges**: Sky gradients — fully feathered transitions (background)
- **Lost-and-found**: Mountain ridges dissolving into atmospheric haze at the far distance
- **Interlocking**: Field patch boundaries — deliberately irregular, not ruler-straight
- **Diffused**: Distant structures and trees, softened by atmospheric perspective

The contrast of edge quality itself creates depth — soft recedes, sharp advances.

#### 9. Golden Ratio Spiral — Eye Flow Path
Arrange the compositional flow so the eye travels in a **spiral**: enters at the warm foreground fields, sweeps through midground trees, arrives at the peak/focal point, then drifts across the dramatic sky. The patchwork pattern itself can reinforce this spiral flow.

### Structure
- **Layered horizontal bands** — sky, mountains, midground trees/structures, foreground fields — each band a distinct zone
- **Diagonal leading lines** — field edges and boundary lines converge toward the focal point, pulling the eye inward
- **Patchwork grid** — the irregular mosaic of field shapes creates a secondary compositional structure, like a quilt overlaid on the landscape
- **Structural punctuation** — dark trees and small farm structures are placed asymmetrically within the midground, breaking the horizontal rhythm and providing vertical counterpoints

### Focal Point
Upper power-point zone (rule of thirds intersection) — where the warmest sky colors meet the mountain silhouette, reinforced by converging field lines. Secondary focal points can be created by placing a single bright field patch (goldenrod or cadmium yellow) or a highlighted structure in the midground.

### Using Reference Images
When a reference photo is provided, pass it directly to Gemini via `--reference-image` so the model can study the actual composition, shapes, and colors. This dramatically improves fidelity — the mountain shape, valley layout, ridge lines, and overall composition will be recognizable from the reference while rendered in the post-impressionist patchwork style.

## Lighting

### Type
**Late dusk / golden hour waning** — the sun has just set or is setting at the horizon behind the viewer (or at a low oblique angle). The landscape is bathed in the warm afterglow with cool shadows beginning to emerge.

### Effects
- Warm ambient glow suffusing the entire scene — the "magic hour" quality
- Fields catching last light read as warm luminous patches (orange, gold, rose)
- Fields in early shadow read as cool muted patches (sage, blue-gray, lavender)
- Tree silhouettes are dark, almost black, their forms simplified to graphic shapes
- Sky carries the full sunset gradient — from warm horizon to cool zenith
- Mountains in deep blue-gray shadow, their forms defined by value rather than color
- No harsh shadows — all light is diffused, soft, atmospheric

### Value Strategy
- **Foreground fields**: Full value range — bright highlights to mid-dark accents
- **Midground**: Mid-tone dominant — moderate value range, trees provide darkest darks
- **Mountains**: Compressed low-to-mid values — dark to medium, never bright
- **Sky**: High-key — lightest values in the painting, with subtle mid-tone warm streaks

## Mood

**Serene, dreamlike tranquility with nostalgic warmth.**

- **Peaceful** — the stillness of rural landscape at day's end, no human figures, only evidence of habitation
- **Nostalgic** — the patchwork fields evoke agricultural heritage, a landscape shaped by generations of labor, seen through the filter of memory
- **Dreamlike** — non-naturalistic color choices (pink fields, orange pastures) create a sense of heightened reality, as if the landscape exists in a state between waking and dreaming
- **Magical** — the dusk light transforms ordinary farmland into a tapestry of jewel-like color, suggesting an enchanted pastoral world
- **Comforting** — the quilt-like mosaic of fields, the small structures nestled among them, the distant protective mountains — all evoke shelter and belonging

### Customization Variables

When generating new images in this style, these elements can be varied while maintaining the style:

- **Season**: Spring (bright greens, cherry blossom pinks), Summer (golden yellows, deep greens), Autumn (rich oranges, burgundy, amber), Winter (muted browns, frost blues, pale gold)
- **Time of day**: Late afternoon (warmer, higher contrast), dusk (balanced warm-cool), twilight (cooler, more purple/violet), early evening (deeper blues, warm horizon line)
- **Crop type**: Wheat fields, vineyards, lavender rows, rice paddies, sunflower fields, terraced hillside farms, wildflower meadows
- **Terrain**: Flat lowland plains, rolling hills, terraced slopes, river valley, coastal farmland, highland plateau
- **Structures**: Farmhouses, barns, silos, stone walls, windmills, fencing, hay bales, irrigation channels
- **Water feature**: Optional — a stream, pond, or canal reflecting the dusk sky, painted in broken scumbled brushwork
- **Atmosphere**: Clear dusk, light ground mist, distant haze, scattered clouds catching sunset color
- **Format**: Single panoramic canvas, or square format for more intimate compositions
