# Graf3 — Spray-Cartoon Character (Street Mascot Burner) Style Guide

> Written from close analysis of reference piece `Graf_Style_02.jpg` (the snarling grey rat with a black bomb-cheese, salmon-pink lightning sparks, orange rim-halo outline, on a charcoal wall).
> Core identity: **flat cell-shaded cartoon-character graffiti** — thick black brushy outlines, a single bright orange/peach rim-halo outline tracing the silhouette, sawtooth-spiked fur edges, salmon-pink lightning-bolt exertion sparks, on a flat dark charcoal wall.

## Style Definition

**Graf3 Spray-Cartoon Character** — a single stylized cartoon creature or mascot rendered as a flat 2D street-mural character: thick confident charcoal-black brushy outlines, a single bright orange-peach hard-edged rim-halo outline tracing the entire outer silhouette, two-to-three flat cell-shaded tonal zones per body-part (NO gradients), sawtooth-spiked fur silhouette implying fur via jagged edges, salmon-pink skin accents (inner ears, paw pads, nose, tail underside), salmon-pink lightning-bolt exertion sparks scattered around joints, and a held prop (food, weapon, object) for narrative hook — all on a flat dark charcoal wall (#1A1A1E). The piece reads as a confident hand-painted graffiti mascot, sitting between marker-art cartooning and bucket-paint mural work.

## Lineage DNA

- **NY graf-character mural lineage** — Cope2's grey rats, T-Kid, Skeme, Sento — confident outline-and-fill cartooning with bold rim-halos
- **Bronx / Brooklyn graf-cartoon tradition** — Mighty Mo, Smith — narrative mascots with held props (food, tools, weapons)
- **European cartoon-mural school** — Os Gemeos, Pjotr, Bart Smeets, Mr. Wany — flat cell-shaded cartooning at mural scale
- **Graf-character convention (1990s-2010s)** — solid black outline + single rim-glow + flat cell-shade fills + comic-book exertion marks
- **Modern dark-wall mascot presentation** — figure isolated on a flat charcoal wall, no street context, gallery-grade

## The Core Technique — RIM-HALO OUTLINE on a flat character

The defining characteristic of Graf3: every character is wrapped in a **single bright orange-peach (#F08C42) rim-halo outline** that traces the entire outer silhouette in a hard-edged, even-width band sitting just outside the primary black outline. This is NOT a soft halo, NOT a gradient glow, NOT a multi-band stack — it is one flat orange ring that gives the character its instantly-recognizable graffiti-mascot pop against the dark wall.

### The two outline layers (in order, outside → inside)

| Layer | Color | Hex | Width (% of figure height) | Position |
|-------|-------|-----|----------------------------|----------|
| **Rim-halo outline** | Bright orange-peach | #F08C42 | 0.8 - 1.4% | Just OUTSIDE the black outline. Traces the full OUTER silhouette only (NOT internal features). Hard-edged, flat color, even width. |
| **Primary outline** | Charcoal-black | #0F0F12 | 2.0 - 3.0% | Wraps the silhouette AND every internal feature. Brushy aerosol-marker line with organic thickness variation — NOT a vector stroke. |

**Rule of thumb**: the orange rim-halo is the single biggest "this is Graf3" tell. Lose it and the piece looks generic. Make it too thick and it overpowers. 1% of figure height is the sweet spot.

### Internal detail lines

- All internal anatomy — mouth, teeth, eye outlines, paw-pad divisions, fur tufts, ear creases, body separations — drawn with the same charcoal-black brushy line, just thinner (~50-70% the weight of the outer outline).
- NEVER use colored interior lines. Black only.

### Rim-halo color swaps (for non-grey palettes)

| Body palette | Rim-halo color | Hex |
|--------------|----------------|-----|
| Grey rat (default) | Bright orange-peach | #F08C42 |
| Olive gremlin | Bright orange-peach | #F08C42 |
| Rust dog (orange body) | Cyan | #5BD4E8 |
| Ink crow (black body) | Cadmium yellow | #F2C530 |
| Toxic bee (yellow body) | Cyan | #5BD4E8 |

The rule: pick a rim-halo color that pops off BOTH the body AND the dark charcoal wall. If the body is already orange/yellow, swap the rim to cyan. If the body is black/dark, swap to yellow.

## The Background — FLAT DARK CHARCOAL WALL, full stop

### The rule (non-negotiable)

The piece sits on a **flat dark charcoal background (#1A1A1E)** — implies a painted wall, but executed flat. Optional very-subtle concrete haze at low contrast.

**NO**:
- No white background (Graf1 is white — Graf3 is dark)
- No brick, no concrete-photo, no plywood, no metal panel
- No environmental context (no alley, no train, no sidewalk, no street furniture)
- No floor, ground, horizon, or sky
- No other graffiti tags or pieces in the background
- No isolated transparent / studio-clean background — the dark wall IS the look

**YES**:
- Flat dark charcoal #1A1A1E with optional very subtle concrete-mottle haze at +/- 8 RGB at most
- Character isolated on the dark wall — no scenery
- Held props can extend into the left or right margin against the same charcoal wall
- The orange rim-halo pops off the dark wall — that's why the wall must stay dark

### Prompt phrase to lock it in

> "Flat dark charcoal wall background (#1A1A1E), no brick, no concrete photo, no plywood, no white, no environmental shadows, no street context, no floor, no horizon, no other graffiti tags or pieces visible, the character is isolated on the flat charcoal wall."

## Subject Philosophy — A single character, not a scene

### Character construction rules

- **One creature**, side-profile or three-quarter view. NOT front-on (front-on flattens the silhouette and loses the graf-mascot read).
- **Exaggerated body language** — hunched shoulders, locked jaw, beady eyes, stress-pose. The character is up to something.
- **A held prop** — almost always. The reference rat has a black bomb-cheese in its mouth and a Swiss-cheese wedge in its paws. Bones, sandwiches, spray cans, tools, weapons, stolen objects all work.
- **Anchored low** — the character sits in the lower-middle band of the canvas, not floating dead-center. Reads as "on the ground" even without a ground plane.
- **Horizontal motion** — the character runs / sneaks / lunges / perches left-to-right across the canvas.

### Subject vocabulary that works

- "snarling rat with cheese chunk" (reference)
- "sneering bulldog with stolen bone"
- "wild-eyed crow with a spray can"
- "pissed-off bee with a hammer"
- "grimy gremlin with a lock-pick"
- "sly fox with a stolen sandwich"
- "scheming pigeon with a slice of pizza"
- "feral cat with a fish skeleton"
- "punk raccoon with a wallet"

### Terms to AVOID in prompts

- "wildstyle", "interlocking", "arrow-tipped" (that is Graf1)
- "chrome", "metallic", "blockbuster", "bevel" (that is Graf2)
- "graffiti font", "letters spelling X"
- "cute", "kawaii", "Pixar", "smooth render", "photorealistic fur"
- "vector clean", "logo style"
- "front-on", "facing camera"

## Linework — Brushy not vector

### Primary outline rules

- **Charcoal-black #0F0F12**, NOT pure black (a hint of cool-blue keeps it from being flat-dead)
- **Brushy aerosol-marker line** — organic thickness variation, slight wobble, occasional small bumps. NOT a uniform vector stroke.
- **Width: 2-3% of figure height** for the outer silhouette outline; 1.2-1.8% for internal detail lines.
- **Continuous and confident** — no hesitation marks, no double lines, no missing segments.
- **Wraps everything** — silhouette, internal anatomy, held objects, every separation.

### Rim-halo outline rules

- **Bright orange-peach #F08C42**, hard-edged, flat color, no gradient.
- **Sits just outside the primary black outline**, even width all around.
- **Traces the OUTER silhouette only** — does NOT trace internal features.
- **Width: ~1% of figure height**, never thicker than the primary black outline.
- **Single continuous ring** — one band, not two, not a soft glow, not a fade.

## Fills — Flat cell-shading, not gradients

### Cell-shade construction

Every body-part is built from **2-3 flat tonal zones**, each a single even color separated by a hard edge:

1. **Midtone** — the main fill color of the body-part (e.g., spray-grey #9A9AA0 for fur)
2. **Shadow** — a darker tone of the same hue, applied as a flat shape on the underside / away-from-light side (under belly, under jaw, far leg, inside ear). Hard edge, no blend.
3. **Highlight** (optional) — a single lighter-tone flat shape on top-facing surfaces (top of head, top of haunch). Used sparingly. Most highlight reading comes from leaving the midtone exposed against the shadow.

### Skin accents (CRITICAL chromatic punch)

The grey body needs a chromatic counter. **Salmon-pink #F2B0B8** is applied to:
- Inner ears
- Nose tip / snout interior
- Paw pads (where visible)
- Tail underside
- Tongue (if mouth is open)

Each pink zone gets a darker mauve-pink #C58088 flat shadow shape. Every Graf3 creature gets at least 3-5 pink skin moments — they are non-negotiable.

### What flat cell-shading is NOT

- NOT airbrushed gradient inside fills
- NOT photoreal texture
- NOT hatching, cross-hatching, or linework shading
- NOT halftone dots
- NOT a noise/grain inside the fill

## Silhouette — The Sawtooth-Fur Technique

### The rule

Fur (or feather) is implied by the **silhouette EDGE being jagged**, NOT by painting individual hairs. Cluster triangular tufts along major contours.

| Body zone | Silhouette treatment |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **Back, head crown, shoulders, haunches, tail base** | Sawtooth-spiked — clusters of 2-5 triangular tufts, varying length 3-12% of local body diameter |
| **Belly, snout, paw pads, inner ears, tail tip** | Smooth-curved silhouette — no spikes |
| **Cheeks, jaw underside** | Mid-spike — smaller scattered tufts, less aggressive than back |

### Why this matters

The reference rat is recognizably furry without a single hair painted on its surface. The interior is flat grey. All the "fur" reads happen at the silhouette edge. Once you understand this, every Graf3 creature gets the same treatment: rough on top, smooth on the belly.

## Accents — Lightning Sparks, Eyes, Teeth, Pink

### Lightning-bolt exertion sparks (the signature accent)

- **Shape**: 3-4-segment zig-zag lightning-bolt, classic comic-book style
- **Color fill**: Salmon-pink #F2B0B8, with a thin charcoal-black outline
- **Quantity**: 3-6 across the figure
- **Placement**: Around joints under stress (shoulders, haunches), behind/around the head, near gripped objects — wherever a comic artist would draw "exertion / impact / kinetic" emphasis
- **Size**: 4-10% of figure height each, varied
- **Purpose**: Replaces the star-burst that other graf-skill styles use. Says "this character is in motion / under stress / dangerous."

### Eye treatment

- **Shape**: Wedge / angled-rectangle angry eyes — half-closed predator stare. NOT round, NOT cute.
- **Fill**: Hard white #FFFFFF
- **Outline**: Charcoal-black #0F0F12 brushy line
- **Pupil**: Often OMITTED entirely for a more menacing all-white look. If present, a small black dot or short hard slit only.

### Tooth treatment

- **Shape**: Hard triangular fangs / blocky chompers, NOT realistic dentition
- **Fill**: Hard white #FFFFFF
- **Outline**: Charcoal-black #0F0F12 brushy line
- **Bite rule**: If the mouth is open or gripping something, lock the teeth ONTO the object — they should bite into it visually, not float near it.

### Skin-accent rule (repeat for emphasis)

Every Graf3 creature gets 3-5 salmon-pink #F2B0B8 skin accent zones (inner ears, nose, paw pads, tail underside, tongue). Without the pink, the piece reads flat-grey-dead.

### What Graf3 does NOT use

- No star-burst sparkles (the lightning-bolt replaces them)
- No drips from the figure
- No overspray mist or aerosol speckle around the edges
- No outer halo glow / radial aura (the rim-halo outline replaces it)
- No 3D drop-block, no bevel, no extrusion (Graf3 is FLAT 2D)

## Composition

### Format

**Horizontal banner** — character runs left-to-right across the canvas.

- **DEFAULT AND ONLY STANDARD**: 16:9 at 3840x2160px (Samsung Frame TV)
- 21:9 / 2:1 acceptable for elongated creatures (snake, dragon)
- 1:1 / 9:16 are off-spec — the cartoon mascot needs horizontal canvas room

### Layout zones (on dark charcoal wall)

```
+----------------------------------------------------+
|                                                    |
|   dark charcoal wall (breathing room)              |
|                                                    |
|       /lightning  /lightning                       |
|      _____.____    +-------+                       |
|     |  ORANGE-RIM   CHARACTER  +---PROP            |
|     |   HALO       (cell-shaded                    |
|     |              flat fills,                     |
|     |              sawtooth fur                    |
|     |              silhouette)                     |
|     +-------------------+----------+               |
|                                                    |
|   dark charcoal wall                               |
+----------------------------------------------------+
              flat dark charcoal #1A1A1E
```

### Placement rules

- **Character fills 70-85% of the width** — leaves 8-15% margin for breathing dark wall
- **Character fills 55-75% of the height** — anchored to the lower-middle band
- **Anchored low** — the visual weight of the character sits in the lower-middle, not dead-center. Implies "on the ground" without painting a ground plane.
- **Held props extend into the margin** — cheese, bones, weapons can break the left or right edge, adding asymmetry
- **Side-profile or three-quarter view** — NEVER front-on
- **Lightning sparks scatter** around the figure inside the rim-halo zone, not in the open dark wall

### Eye flow

1. Lock onto the white predator eyes (highest contrast point)
2. Read the silhouette — recognize the creature instantly
3. Follow the outline-and-rim-halo edge around the figure
4. Pick up the held prop (cheese / bone / weapon)
5. Notice the lightning sparks scattered around the joints
6. Linger on the salmon-pink skin moments (inner ears, paw pads)

## Mood

**Cheeky, menacing, kinetic — a street-mascot up to no good.**

- **Cheeky** — the held prop tells a tiny story (the rat stole the cheese, the dog stole the bone)
- **Menacing** — predator eyes, locked-jaw teeth, hunched aggressive pose
- **Kinetic** — lightning sparks, sneaky low-prowl pose, tail-in-motion
- **Bold** — comic-book outlines, hard-edge cell-shade, no softness
- **Slightly lawless** — this is a graffiti character, not a children's-book illustration

### Mood vocabulary

- "snarling", "sneering", "scheming", "wild-eyed", "grimy", "punk", "feral", "sly"
- "low and prowling", "perched and watchful", "hunched and locked"
- "stolen prop", "contraband", "swiped"

### Mood to avoid

- "cute", "kawaii", "adorable", "sweet"
- "polished mascot", "corporate logo", "vector"
- "realistic", "anatomically correct", "photoreal fur"
- "horror", "gore", "scary"
